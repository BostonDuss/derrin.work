What's next

Different rooms, same job. Here's the next room.

People keep telling me AI is a new landscape. It's a new room.

Every program on this site had elements nobody on the team had ever executed. Nobody had built a shoppable window. Nobody had templated four thousand pages. Five agencies were running a process none of them had ever mapped. I've spent fifteen years being the person in the room where nobody knows how yet. And in AI that isn't a phase you get through, it's the permanent condition. That's not a challenge I'm bracing for. It's the thing I'm actually good at.

And I've already done a version of it. At ServiceNow I taught myself the AI platform and drove adoption across studio, publishing, and engineering. That's the work I want more of: not building the model, but figuring out what it's for. Which tools actually make someone's day better, and how we'd know if they did.

Concretely, what I want to architect is process: systems thinking applied to agent-first workflows, with human-in-the-loop integration at the points where judgment actually has to sit. Same instinct as the swimlane map, pointed at a chain where some of the participants aren't people. I hold an AI Strategist and Builder certification from the Harvard Data Science Review.

Adoption breaks at the seams: the places between teams that nobody owns. That's the thing I count.

And I'd add one more: the hat where I say no and make yes cost something is a safety hat. The discipline that took two-thirds of the scope out of a platform release is the same discipline that asks whether a feature should ship, not just whether it can. In this field I think that's the more important question, and I'd rather work somewhere that agrees.
