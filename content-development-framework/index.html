Arnold Worldwide, 2015–17  ·  National Association of Realtors

Content Development Framework

The errors weren't in anyone's work. They were in the seams between everyone's work. And nobody had ever counted how many seams there were.

Agency Arnold Worldwide
Client National Association of Realtors
Years 2015–17

The situation

NAR's social content moved through five agencies: Arnold leading creative, Havas leading media, and three Havas Media sister-agencies handling trafficking, social management, and analytics. That meant there were multiple leads and therefore no one who owned the whole, and a steady drip of production errors nobody could trace to a cause.

My role

I had no authority over four of the five agencies. Arnold led creative; Havas led media; the process itself belonged to nobody. So I took the only role that was open: the person trying to understand it. What happened because I was there is we found out the errors weren't in anyone's work. They were in the seams between everyone's work, and no one, including me, had ever counted how many seams there were.

What I did

Mapped every agency's process by asking, not auditing. I went to each team and made the frame explicit up front: I'm not here to find someone to point at, I'm here to find where the discrepancies are. That wasn't a courtesy. Four of those agencies didn't work for me, and a defensive team hands you the process they wish they ran instead of the one they actually run.

Found the seams. Lowest-common-denominator specs were never explicitly provided to anyone, so assets got built to specs that didn't clear every placement. And the copy team had never been told that a partner agency appended short URLs to post copy, which blew the character counts they'd been writing to. Two teams, both correct, one broken output.

Did the math nobody had done. Remapped end to end, the process took about 15 days. We were producing content a week at a time. The process was longer than the cycle it served. That meant everyone in the chain had been failing on schedule, structurally, since the start of this social activation.

Expanded the cycle instead of compressing the process. The obvious move is to squeeze 15 days into 5. I proposed the opposite: concept and produce a month's worth of content per development cycle. It required the social team to flesh the calendar out a month ahead, and they were willing.

Held the line in the room. Leadership had me present the remap to all five agencies. Senior people asked who was responsible. I framed every one of them as a miss by the whole team and put formal communication channels forward as the fix. That was the answer that let five agencies adopt it instead of four agencies defending themselves.

What happened

Adopted by all five agencies, including the four I had no authority over

Extended to other client accounts, which is the actual proof it was a framework and not a fix

The repeat errors stopped, and the time we'd spent correcting delivered assets stopped being spent

Trackable milestones and identified parallel paths, so the chain became legible to the people inside it

Reactive capacity went up, not down. The tradeoff should have been timeliness. You can't newsjack a calendar built a month out. But because Creative sits at the front of the chain and was now producing in batches, they had room to absorb ad-hoc requests with minimal downstream impact. We bought back the thing we were supposed to be giving away.

You cannot see a seam you haven't drawn.

What I'd do differently

I only counted the process after it had already failed enough times to force an investigation. Nobody knew how many people were in that chain. And I'd been working inside it. That map should have existed on day one, not month two. Any time work crosses more than two organizations, the seams are where it breaks, and you cannot see a seam you haven't drawn. I don't wait for the errors to tell me anymore.
