AKQA, 2017–21  ·  Fallout 76 charity activation

Helmets for Habitat

Both creative leads were gone within weeks of greenlight. Our execution partner had no execution plan. Neither became the client's problem.

Agency AKQA
Project Fallout 76 charity activation
With Bethesda, HYPEBEAST, Microsoft
Beneficiary Habitat for Humanity
Year 2017–21
Role Senior Producer

The situation

A game about rebuilding civilization together needed an activation that meant it: 76 custom-painted helmets, auctioned for Habitat for Humanity. It was pitched to the client before anyone had figured out how to actually make it, on a budget that couldn't move.

My role

Within weeks of greenlight, the Creative Director and ACD who invented this project were both gone: one to maternity leave, one to another account. Then the partner we'd bet the entire execution on turned out to have no execution plan. What happened because I was there is neither of those issues became the client's problem.

What I did

Rebuilt the team without losing the thread. Ran the transition brief with the departing creatives myself, then selected the incoming team against bandwidth rather than availability, backfilling their other projects so nobody arrived already underwater. Re-ran the billing analysis and scenario-planned the burn through year-end so the budget survived the handoff.

Supplied the operating system our partner didn't have. HYPEBEAST's promotions team was young, and we were among the first large-scale brand partners they'd taken on. When it became clear the plan for artist briefing, shipping, cataloging, tracking, and risk mitigation didn't exist, I built it rather than escalate and stall: tracking systems, guidelines, and the cross-org communication cadence.

Absorbed a mid-flight scope change without repricing the work. Microsoft's late interest added co-branded assets, a brand identity HYPEBEAST's system couldn't stretch to cover, a digital lookbook, and two gallery showcases. The second was added days out. I negotiated them down from every helmet to a shipped subset across NYC and LA, and coordinated the logistics directly.

Protected the team from the project. This one had late nights. I planned their other work around it, held off the rest of the studio's requests for their time, and went to leadership for comp days when it was over.

What happened

$200K+ raised for Habitat for Humanity

40+ artists across the globe producing custom pieces, plus a surprise donation of extra helmets from the client's production partners, who wanted in

Delivered on budget within ~1% of a $150K earmark, despite a full creative team turnover

Risk padding did its job: a two-week customs seizure in Berlin cost a separate shoot and landed the final four pieces two days before the auction site went live, with no impact to timeline or cost

~$2K of unused OOP redirected into collectible posters, a printed lookbook, custom packaging, and personalized notes, which auction winners turned into organic unboxing content for free

Client stakeholders bid on and won helmets themselves

I ask partners to walk me through how they've run the thing before, not whether they can.

What I'd do differently

I diligenced HYPEBEAST on audience fit and reach, and got that right. I didn't diligence them on operational capability, and spent the project building the scaffolding I'd assumed came with the partner. I now ask partners to walk me through how they've run the thing before, not whether they can.
